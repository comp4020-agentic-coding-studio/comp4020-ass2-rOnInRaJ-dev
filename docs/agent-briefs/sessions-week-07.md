## What this page must do

Cover the four studio hours where each team sizes its own spar against
`R-10` and gets a structural mass out of CAD, before the field's load-rig test
on a spare club wing (a different, generic wing, not their own). No other page
does the own-design spar sizing.

## Where the prose goes

One new section, `## In the studio`, inserted between the
"This week works on `R-10`." line and the `## At the field` heading. ~300
words, two paragraphs: workshop hours, then lab hours.

## Must say

- Workshop hours: each team sizes a spar for its own wing against the **3.5
  g** limit load named in `R-10`, choosing and defending a material and
  section against the mass it costs — the wing-structure line in the ledger
  (**310 g**) is the budget it has to fit inside.
- Lab hours: the team predicts tip deflection at limit load from its own beam
  calculation and records it in the ledger before the field slot, so the
  field's measured number (on the club wing) has something of the team's own
  to be compared against conceptually, even though the two wings are not the
  same wing.
- Name `R-10` explicitly, matching the spec bullet already on this page.
- One sentence that the structural mass entered into CAD this week is what
  the ledger's wing-structure line should now read as sourced or measured,
  not estimated.

## The failure story

Story 7 (spar sized for shear, failed in bending) as a short warning aside in
the workshop-hours paragraph — framed as the check a team runs against its own
spar choice before it is cut: which load case did you size for, and does it
cover the one that actually governs.

## Must not

- Must not restate the lecture's shear-versus-bending explanation or the
  worked example in full — refer to the mistake, do not re-teach it.
- Must not describe the load-rig procedure on the spare club wing — that is
  the fixed "At the field" block below this section.
- Must not touch centre of gravity or tail sizing — week 6.
- Must not do interface control or failure-mode mapping — week 8.

## Voice notes

- Workshop-hours paragraph should read as a design decision with a number
  attached; lab-hours paragraph as a prediction logged before a test, in
  keeping with the course's predict-then-check rhythm.
- No suspense on the aside — one flat sentence naming the check, not a retold
  story.
