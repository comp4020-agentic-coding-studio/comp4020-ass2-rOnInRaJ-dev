## What this page must do
Teach flight testing as a designed experiment — one question per flight, what to instrument, and how tuning differs on your own airframe versus a club trainer — using the course design-point numbers as the one worked illustration of predicted-versus-measured. No other page owns this method.

## Where the prose goes
Two to three paragraphs between "**By the end:**" and "## Outline". Target ~300 words: paragraph 1 (~100 words) on one question per flight and why two questions answer neither; paragraph 2 (~120 words) on instrumentation — what to log and what it's compared against, using the course design-point figures as the worked example; paragraph 3 (~80 words) on why tuning a controller on a club trainer doesn't transfer, and the flight readiness review as the last gate before week 12.

## Must say
- Requirement IDs: R-03, R-05, R-07 (the `reviewWeeks[11].covers` entries).
- The worked predicted-vs-measured illustration, using the course design point (not any team's real aircraft, and say so explicitly): predicted stall speed 7.7 m/s against the 8.5 m/s cap (R-03); predicted cruise power 60 W against the 70 W cap (R-05); predicted endurance around twenty-six minutes against a twenty-minute floor (R-07) — spell "twenty-six minutes" and "twenty-minute" in words, not digits, since minutes are not a ledger-tracked unit.
- State plainly that these three are design-point numbers and a team's own airframe will differ — echo the studio page's own line about not using the course design point for your own prediction, without quoting it verbatim.
- Instrumentation: name that pack voltage and current, airspeed, and time are the minimum log, and that log rate matters as much as what's logged (an under-sampled log misses the transient that answers the one question).
- Tuning: state once, plainly, that a controller tuned on a club trainer does not transfer to a different airframe — different mass, different control surfaces, different behaviour.
- Flight readiness review: the last formal gate before week 12; nothing is flown at the fly-off without it.
