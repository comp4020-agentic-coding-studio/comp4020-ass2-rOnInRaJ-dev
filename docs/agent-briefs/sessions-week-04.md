## What this page must do
Get a team's own wing area sized from its own mass and stall-speed target, and set a measured lift-to-drag ratio next to the calculated one with an account of the gap. This is the only page where a team's own aerodynamic prediction meets a real flown measurement.

## Where the prose goes
A new `## In the studio` section, between the existing `This week works on...` line and `## At the field`. About 300 words in two paragraphs: workshop hours (running the wing-area sizing calculation from the team's own mass and the stall-speed cap) and lab hours (weighing the team's current airframe stock so the mass feeding that calculation is sourced, not guessed).

## Must say
- The requirement IDs already in the file — R-02, R-03 — are fixed. Do not restate the "This week works on" line.
- The wing area follows from the team's own all-up mass and the stall-speed cap of 8.5 m/s (`thresholds.stallSpeedCapMs`) — this figure may be cited since it is in the ledger's allowed set. No other aero figures should be invented.
- The field objective already fixed in the file (measuring a club trainer's glide angle from a fixed release height, backing out a lift-to-drag ratio) is not to be restated — refer to it once as the source of the "measured" half of the comparison the spec requires.
- Workshop hours: the wing-sizing calculation itself, on the team's own numbers. Lab hours: weighing current airframe stock and components against the mass estimate that calculation depends on.

## The failure story
Not story 4 here — it belongs to the lecture, which owns the wrong-Reynolds-number worked example. The studio's own "account of the gap" (required by its spec line) may note that a mismatched Reynolds number is one candidate reason measured and calculated lift-to-drag differ, stated as a plain fact, not a retelling of the story.

## Must not
- Must not retell the wrong-Reynolds-number story in narrative form — the lecture owns it.
- Must not explain Reynolds number as a concept, or discuss published polars in general — the lecture's job.
- Must not cite battery, pack or endurance figures — week 5's territory.
- Must not restate the field objective, predict, weather fallback, or safety lines already fixed in the file.

## Voice notes
- The gap between measured and calculated lift-to-drag is the deliverable — write toward explaining a gap, not toward a clean result.
- No triumphant tone if the numbers happen to agree; that is luck, not a system working.
