## What this page must do
Teach the difference between a wish and a requirement, and open the mass ledger as a live discipline. This is the only page that defines what makes a requirement testable, and the only lecture that introduces the ledger's rule that every line is an estimate with a method behind it.

## Where the prose goes
New prose between the `**By the end:**` line and `## Outline`. About 300 words in two paragraphs: the first uses the failure story to show what a wish looks like next to a requirement; the second explains the ledger's discipline without listing any of the actual mass-budget line items — that is the studio and assessment's job to populate.

## Must say
- The course requirement set, R-01 to R-14, described as a floor: it sets the mass cap, the power budget and the disposal rule, not what any one team's aircraft does.
- `R-01` cited as the example of a requirement with a threshold (1800 g), an owner (Structures) and a verification method (weighed, flight-ready, on the lab scale).
- A requirement needs three things: a threshold, an owning subsystem, and a way to verify it — this mirrors the shape of every entry in `requirements.ts`.
- The ledger opens this week: every line is an estimate with a method behind it, not yet a measurement. No specific mass values need citing here.

## The failure story
Story 2 ("a decent distance"). Use it as the central worked example: a mission sentence everyone nodded at in week 2, until someone asked for the number in metres and got three different answers from three teammates. This is the exact moment a wish becomes a requirement — make it the spine of that paragraph, not a passing aside.

## Must not
- Must not enumerate the mass budget's line items (wing structure, fuselage, battery, and so on) — that is the ledger's content, populated by teams, not narrated here.
- Must not discuss the configuration trade or scoring criteria — that is week 3's page.
- Must not discuss aerofoils, Reynolds number, or wing sizing — week 4.
- Must not describe the field measurement of circuit time — that belongs to the week 2 studio page.

## Voice notes
- "Decent" is the word to make fun of, gently, by being specific about what replaces it.
- State the three things a requirement needs as a flat list of nouns, not a rhetorical question.
