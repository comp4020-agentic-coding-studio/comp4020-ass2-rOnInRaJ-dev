## What this page must do
Establish the course's central idea — the aircraft is a system of five subsystems trading against one shared budget — before any subsystem has done anything yet. This is the only page that introduces the five subsystems as a concept and the only page that gets to explain why week 1 is flying, not designing.

## Where the prose goes
One block of new prose between the `**By the end:**` line and the `## Outline` heading. About 300 words, in two paragraphs: the first opens with the failure story as the hook for what "system" means; the second names the five subsystems, notes how they map onto a four-person team, and closes on why this course starts with a flight, not a drawing.

## Must say
- The five subsystems from `requirements.ts`: Aero, Structures, Propulsion & Power, Avionics & Control, Operations — name all five. They are the owners the requirement set (R-01 to R-14) is written against.
- The four team roles from Policies (chief engineer; aero and structures; avionics and electrical; test lead) map onto five subsystems — state plainly that one role necessarily covers two subsystems, without asserting which pairing. That is left to teams to settle.
- The mass cap, cited once: `R-01`, 1800 g. Reference it only as the number the whole course argues about — do not break it into lines (week 2 opens the ledger; do not front-run it).

## The failure story
Story 1 (pilot-induced oscillation). Use it as the opening worked example: the loop between a student's hand, the transmitter and the aircraft is itself a system, with a lag in it, and that is what "system" means here before it means "five subsystems." Land it briefly — enough to make the point, not a full account of the flight.

## Must not
- Must not define what makes a requirement, or discuss R-01 to R-14 as a set — that is week 2's page.
- Must not open or list any mass-budget line item (wing, fuselage, battery, and so on) — those appear from week 2 onward.
- Must not discuss trade studies or configuration choice (week 3) or aerofoils and Reynolds number (week 4).
- Must not narrate the field flight itself, the buddy-box mechanics, or the thirty-second objective — that belongs to the week 1 studio page.

## Voice notes
- Open on the loop-with-lag idea plainly. No "welcome" and no scene-setting about the semester ahead.
- Define "system" by what it does — things hand each other numbers — not by analogy.
- No sentence should describe the course's ambitions; describe what one subsystem hands another instead.
