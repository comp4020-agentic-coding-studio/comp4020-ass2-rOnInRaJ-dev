# The twelve failure stories — one per week, no repeats

Each is a real kind of model-aviation failure. The week's studio prose works
one of them through: what happened, what the team believed beforehand, which
requirement would have caught it. Do not move a story to another week and do
not invent a thirteenth.

1. Pilot-induced oscillation. The student over-corrects on the buddy box, the
   instructor takes it back, and the aircraft is fine — the point is that the
   loop between eye, hand and aircraft is itself a system with a lag in it.
2. "A decent distance." A mission statement everyone nodded at in week 2, until
   someone asked how far, in metres, and three team members gave three answers.
3. The reverse-engineered trade study. The team had already decided on a
   fixed-wing; the criteria weights arrived afterwards and, remarkably, agreed.
4. The wrong Reynolds number. A published aerofoil polar taken at a Reynolds
   number ten times ours; the real wing stalls well before the graph says.
5. Brownout. The BEC sags when four servos move at once, the flight controller
   reboots at altitude, and the aircraft flies itself into the ground unpowered.
6. Aft centre of gravity. The aircraft feels wonderfully light on the first two
   circuits and departs on the third.
7. Spar failure at the root. The joiner was sized for shear and then loaded in
   bending, which is a different number entirely.
8. A reversed aileron servo. Bench-tested with the wing off, discovered on the
   take-off roll.
9. Mass creep. Every line item five per cent over, none of them worth arguing
   about on its own, all of them together eating the reserve by CDR.
10. Failsafe set to hold last stick position. The deliberate transmitter-off
    test at altitude puts the aircraft into a spiral, exactly as configured.
11. A prop strike on landing, because the centre-of-gravity check was run
    without the payload fitted and the real aircraft lands nose-down.
12. A pack returned puffed, because it sat fully charged in a car boot for a
    fortnight over the mid-semester break.
