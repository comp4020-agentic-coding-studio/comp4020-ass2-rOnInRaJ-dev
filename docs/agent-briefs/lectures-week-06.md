## What this page must do

Make the argument that stability is a mass problem before it is a geometry
problem: you cannot size a tail or place a centre of gravity range without the
mass week 5 fixed, and this week is where that number gets used for the first
time. No other page carries this specific handover from week 5's power/mass
work into week 6's tail sizing.

## Where the prose goes

Two insertion points, ~300 words total.

1. One paragraph (~150 words) between `**By the end:**` and `## Outline`:
   opens with the handover from week 5 (pack mass fixed, wing mass fixed in
   week 4, now the tail has to be sized against both) and states plainly why
   an aft centre of gravity is dangerous rather than merely wrong.
2. A short block (~150 words) after the `## Outline` list, carrying the
   failure story and a one-line forward pointer to week 7 (the structure that
   carries this same aircraft).

## Must say

- The mass this week works from is the allocated mass from the ledger
  (**1560 g**), against the cap (**1800 g**) — say once that this number was
  not available before week 5 closed the power system.
- Name `R-11` (centre of gravity between 28% and 34% of mean aerodynamic
  chord — spell the range in words, do not print it as a percentage figure)
  as the requirement this week's tail-volume work is aimed at closing.
- Name `R-02` (wing loading at or under **45 g/dm²**, the design point sits at
  **40.6 g/dm²**) once, as the other requirement the CAD model will be
  checked against once it returns a mass.
- One sentence on what "CAD I" means concretely: the model is the thing the
  ledger will read a mass line out of from this week on, replacing an
  estimate with a modelled value.
- One sentence distinguishing static margin (a feel, forward of neutral point)
  from the CG range in `R-11` (a stated band) — they are related, not the same
  thing, and the lecture should not let them blur.

## The failure story

Story 6 (aft centre of gravity: the aircraft feels light on the first two
circuits and departs on the third) as a worked example under the static
margin bullet — use it to make the point that an aft CG does not announce
itself early, which is exactly why `R-11` is a checked band and not a
preference.

## Must not

- Must not repeat week 4's wing-area or stall-speed derivation, or its
  Reynolds-number content — that page owns lift, drag and wing sizing.
- Must not restate week 5's cruise-power or pack-sizing numbers beyond the
  one mass figure needed for the handover sentence.
- Must not describe CAD II or getting structural mass into the ledger — that
  is week 7's lecture.
- Must not describe the three-position flight demonstration in any detail —
  that is the session page's field objective, already written.

## Voice notes

- The handover sentence (week 5's mass enables week 6's tail) should read as
  one plain causal claim, not as a recap of last week's lecture.
- Keep the CG bullet clinical — no metaphor about balance or trust, just the
  static-margin-versus-departure mechanism.
