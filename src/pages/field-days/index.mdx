# Field days

Every week's studio has a field objective, and every team flies it in its own
booked slot. Slots are not timetabled for you. Your team books them, around
four people's timetables and the club's availability, and the booking record is
part of your [flight logbook](/assessments/flight-logbook/) mark.

Mount Sludge Model Aircraft Club lends this course its field, its instructors
and its safety officer. None of that is owed to you.

## Booking

- Slots open three weeks ahead and close **48 h** before the slot starts. A
  booking made later than that does not exist, however good the weather is.
- Windows run Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday, in three-hour blocks from first
  light. Two teams per block, no more, because there is one instructor.
- One club lab person is on duty in every block. They answer questions and they
  hold the go/no-go. They are not there to fly your aircraft for you.
- Each team books **at least one slot per teaching week**. Weeks 10, 11 and 12
  need two, and every team discovers this in week 10.
- Cancel more than 48 h out and it costs you nothing. Cancel inside 48 h, or
  simply not turn up, and the slot is gone: another team could have had it.

## Go, or no-go

The safety officer on duty makes the call, on the day, at the field. It is not
a negotiation and it is not appealable to course staff.

- Wind across the strip above the day's stated limit: no-go for students. The
  instructor may still fly a demonstration.
- Rain on the airframe, or a wet strip: no-go for anything with exposed
  electronics.
- Cloudbase below the circuit height, or visibility down: no-go.
- Mowing, stock, or another club event on the field: no-go until it clears.

Every week's studio page states a **weather fallback** — the version of that
week's objective you run when the answer is no-go. Read it before you drive out.

## On the day

- Your name is on the flight line, or you do not hold a transmitter.
- Packs are logged out at the start and logged back in at the end. This is
  `R-14`, and it is the one requirement on this course with no engineering
  content and no flexibility.
- Whatever you predicted goes in the log **before** you fly, not after.
- If something breaks, photograph it where it lies before you pick it up.

## Afterwards

Within 48 h, every student on the team writes their own logbook entry: what was
booked, what the weather did, what was predicted, what was observed, and what
you now think is true that you did not think before. Entries written a fortnight
later read like entries written a fortnight later, and they are marked as such.
