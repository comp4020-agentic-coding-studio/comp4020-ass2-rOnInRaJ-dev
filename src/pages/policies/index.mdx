# Policies and support

## Workload

This is a twelve-unit intensive, and it is about double an ordinary six-unit
course. If that does not fit your semester, it is better to know now than in
week 9 with a half-built wing.

| Component | Hours |
| --- | --- |
| Lecture | 2 |
| Studio: workshop and lab | 4 |
| Team field slot | 3 |
| Team build, design and ledger | 6 |
| Your own logbook, quizzes and reading | 3 |

**Total:** 18 h per teaching week, for twelve weeks.

The build hours are not evenly spread. Weeks 8 to 10 run over; weeks 2 and 3
run under. Plan for the peak, not the average.

## Teams

Teams are four people, fixed from week 2, with four roles: chief engineer, aero
and structures, avionics and electrical, test lead. Roles rotate once, at the
critical design review in week 9, so nobody leaves having done one job.

Group marks are moderated by peer review at the end of semester. A team member
whose contribution is consistently rated well below the rest of their team can
receive a mark below the team's. This is not used to settle personality
disputes; it is used when work did not happen.

If a team is failing as a team, say so in week 6, not week 11. It can be fixed
in week 6.

## Late work, and extensions

Written work is late after the stated time and loses five per cent of the
available marks per calendar day. After seven days it is not marked.

The fly-off is a fixed event and cannot be late. If your aircraft is not ready
in week 12, you fly what you have, and the reasoning in your logbook is worth
more than a tidy aircraft.

Extensions are granted before the deadline, not after, and are arranged with
the convenor. Ask early; asking early is never the thing that costs you.

## Safety

Everything on this site about flying is a **Mount Sludge Model Aircraft Club
rule or a course rule**. Slop University and the club are both fictional, and
nothing here is a statement of anybody's law, in any country. If you fly a real
aircraft anywhere, find out what actually applies where you are.

Within the course: the safety officer's go/no-go is final, the flight line is
the flight line, and lithium polymer packs are charged in the workshop bay and
nowhere else.

## Academic integrity, and what is yours

You will use tools that generate things — CAD, code, controller tuning,
writing. Use them. Then be able to say what the tool produced, what you
changed, and why.

The line this course cares about is simple: a number you cannot derive is not
your number. If your ledger says the wing is 310 g and nobody on your team can
say where that came from, it does not matter which tool produced it.

## Getting help

Workshop questions go to the lab technician during a studio. Field questions go
to the club, at the field. Everything else goes to the convenor, and the fastest
route is the ten minutes after the Monday lecture.
