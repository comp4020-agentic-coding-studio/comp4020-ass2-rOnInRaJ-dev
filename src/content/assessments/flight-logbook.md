## The brief

> Keep a logbook the way you would keep one if somebody else had to work out,
> from it alone, why the aircraft did what it did.

This is your own mark, not your team's. You write it, you sign it, and it
covers the whole semester: field slots booked, weather calls, what you set out
to test, what actually happened.

The entries that carry the mark are the post-mortems. Something will go wrong —
the centre of gravity will be aft, a pack will sag, a servo will be reversed, a
prop will strike on landing. A post-mortem takes that event back to a cause and
says what you changed. Three of those, done properly, outweigh twenty entries
that say the flight went fine.

## What you submit

A single logbook file in your team repository, under your own name, with dated
entries and your booking record. Photographs and flight logs are welcome and do
not substitute for the reasoning.

Holistically, against the statement below.
