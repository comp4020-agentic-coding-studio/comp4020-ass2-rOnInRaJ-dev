## The brief

> Fly the aircraft you said you were going to build, do the thing you said it
> would do, and show us the numbers you predicted in week 4.

The fly-off runs all day in week 12 at Mount Sludge. Every team flies the
mission it wrote in week 3 with the payload fitted, and every team flies its
signature capability — the one thing this aircraft does that the others do not.

Effectiveness is the mission: did it fly, did it carry, did it come back. It is
not a measure of how impressive the aircraft looked on the bench.

Creativity of capabilities is the signature. Past shapes have included a
mass-fraction contest entry, slowest stable flight, maximum glide range from a
fixed release height, and a precision drop. The one that scores is the one your
team chose deliberately, designed around from week 4, and can demonstrate on the
day.

The systems-engineering row is the same thirty marks it has been all semester.
This is where predicted-versus-measured lands: a team that missed its endurance
prediction and can explain the gap scores above a team that hit it by accident.

## What you submit

- the aircraft, flying, at the fly-off
- the final ledger and the verification matrix, both closed out
- a short case study: one thing that went wrong this semester, taken back to its
  cause, in the shape used in week 12's lecture
- the packs, returned discharged to the club bin, closing `R-14`
