This week works on `R-01`, `R-09`, `R-10`.

## In the studio

**Workshop hours:** Workshop hours run right up to the review, not before it, because whatever is not physically built yet cannot be weighed, and CDR baselines what is in the room, not what the team planned to have finished. If the tail assembly is still in clamps at the start of the session, it stays in clamps until it is done, and the review sees whatever mass line that leaves behind. Roles also rotate this week: the chief engineer, the aero and structures lead, the avionics and electrical lead, and the test lead all move to a different seat. This is a course rule, not something this team's studio chooses to do — it means the person defending the mass ledger at CDR is not necessarily the person who has owned it since week 3, and that is deliberate. `R-10` is also baselined this week: the spar's layup is what CDR freezes, not tested again until week 10 re-confirms it on the bench.

**Lab hours:** Lab hours are the CDR meeting itself. Every ledger line gets presented tagged estimated, sourced, or measured, and nothing may still say estimated for a part that is sitting on the bench. This week's lecture covers the shape of a change-control record — reason, re-check, record — and any line that has moved since the week 5 preliminary design review baseline gets one opened against it here, in the meeting, not written up afterwards from memory. This is where the mass-creep audit happens. The course design point allocates 310 g to the wing structure, 240 g to the fuselage structure, 85 g to the tail structure, 185 g to propulsion, 230 g to the battery, 120 g to avionics, 90 g to servos, 250 g to payload and 50 g to consumables — an allocated 1560 g against the 1800 g cap, a 240 g reserve. None of those lines needs to be wrong by much. A wing structure a little heavier than its own estimate, a fuselage a little heavier than its own estimate, a battery swapped for one with a slightly heavier connector — five per cent over on each line, and no single number is worth raising a hand about. Added together against a 240 g reserve, they are worth raising a hand about. The review checks the sum, not each line in isolation, which is the only way this kind of creep gets caught before it costs a requirement.

The same session preps the scale and the log sheet for next week's field slot — the ledger's predicted assembled mass gets written down now, sealed, so the field weigh-in is checked against a number nobody can quietly adjust after seeing the scale.

## At the field

Your team books its own slot for this week — see [Field days](/field-days/) for
the rules and the windows. Every team's slot carries the same objective.

- **Field objective:** Weigh every finished component on the club scale and close the mass ledger against `R-01` and `R-09` with the aircraft in front of you.
- **Owner:** Structures
- **Predict:** Predict the assembled mass from your ledger before anything goes on the scale. The gap is the mark.
- **Weather fallback:** Weighing happens indoors, so the field slot converts to a workshop slot and the scale comes with it.
- **Safety:** Nothing with a battery connected goes on the scale. Packs are weighed separately, disconnected, and logged out.
