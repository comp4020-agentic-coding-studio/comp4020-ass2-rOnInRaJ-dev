This week works on `R-01`, `R-02`, `R-07`, `R-08`.

## In the studio

The review pack you present in the first studio is three documents: your numbered requirement set, your ledger at version one, dated and signed, and the configuration trade showing what you chose and what you rejected. The workshop hours assemble the pack, and most of that time goes into the requirement set: you list your own requirements against the course requirements `R-01`, `R-02`, `R-07` and `R-08`, and for each one you write the same three parts the lecture describes — a threshold, an owner, a verification method — because a requirement without all three does not survive being read out loud in a review. A requirement that says "the aircraft should be efficient" does not survive that reading, because the panel's only job this week is to ask, for every line, what number, whose job, checked how — and a team that cannot answer gets sent back to workshop hours to fix the sentence, not the aircraft. Every estimate in the ledger goes in with its method: how you arrived at it, what figure you drew it from, why that figure was the right one to use. Balsa from a supplier's weight chart. Spar depth from a bending-stress calculation. Battery capacity from the pack's datasheet. If you do not know, you state the assumption and flag it, because an unflagged assumption discovered at the review costs more than a flagged one caught in workshop hours. The trade study documents the configuration you chose — fixed-wing or quad — and shows the one you rejected, both scored against your own weighted criteria, with the weights dated before the scores so the review panel can check the order for themselves. Nothing in the pack is left unsourced or unexplained; a blank cell in the ledger is a worse mark than a wrong number with a method attached, because a wrong number with a method can be fixed and a blank cell means nobody looked.

The lab hours make the ledger real rather than merely plausible. You and the lab technician weigh samples of balsa, carbon tube and composite fabric on the same scale that will weigh your finished aircraft in later weeks. Sample stock is never the exact piece that ends up in your airframe — it is the same grade, from the same supplier, off the same shelf the technician restocks weekly — so the sourced figure is a good figure, not a guaranteed one, and the ledger's method column says which is true of every line. A line that read "balsa spar, estimated" becomes a length of balsa on that scale, with the actual reading written next to it and the estimate struck through rather than deleted, so the review panel can see the gap between what you guessed and what the material actually weighs. Structures goes from estimated to sourced, line by line, for as much of the mass budget as you have physical stock for this early. The ledger baseline is locked at version one, dated at the end of week 3. After this, a change to mass is not a discovery — it is a decision, and whoever makes it names the reason in the ledger's own history, the same way the review panel will ask for it later.

## At the field

Your team books its own slot for this week — see [Field days](/field-days/) for
the rules and the windows. Every team's slot carries the same objective.

- **Field objective:** Fly a club quad and a club fixed-wing trainer back to back on comparable packs, and log the difference in flight time.
- **Owner:** Aero
- **Predict:** Predict the ratio between the two endurances from disc loading alone, before either aircraft leaves the ground.
- **Weather fallback:** Wind above the quad's limit: fly the fixed-wing only, take the quad figure from the club log, and flag it in your review as second-hand.
- **Safety:** The quad and the fixed-wing do not share the circuit. One is on the ground before the other launches.

The field objective feeds the trade study directly: the ratio you predicted from disc loading alone is the number the review pack compares against what the two aircraft actually did back to back, on comparable packs, in the same wind. Whichever way that comparison lands, it goes into the pack as evidence, not as an afterthought explaining a decision that was really made in week 1 by whoever already had a preference.
