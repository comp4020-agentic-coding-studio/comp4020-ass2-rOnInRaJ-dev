This week works on `R-03`, `R-05`, `R-07`.

## In the studio

Before the field slot, the team writes down the one question this flight will answer and the predicted number it expects. Not a guess — the predicted value, from your own ledger, or from a calculation the team can explain. Write it on the bench so you know what the log is compared against when you read it afterwards.

The aircraft goes on the bench for a final centre-of-gravity check in its actual flight-ready configuration. This is the weight and balance with the battery fitted, the servo pack attached, the antenna run, everything that flies. A CG check signed off weeks ago without the real flight setup can still be wrong, and it shows up on landing — a nose-in prop strike because the aircraft lands heavier than the bench check assumed. Check the CG again, where it lives.

After the flight, read the log in the room. For each of `R-03`, `R-05` and `R-07`, you measured something and predicted something else. Every gap gets a written reason. Reading the current trace for the moment stall onset shows up is different from eyeballing a top number and moving on — you are comparing your prediction against the flight, not the flight against some vague expectation. Did the stall come early because the wing is smaller than your drawings show? Did cruise power sit lower because the propeller is more efficient in the real air than the thrust model assumed? Write the gap and the reason beside it.

## At the field

Your team books its own slot for this week — see [Field days](/field-days/) for
the rules and the windows. Every team's slot carries the same objective.

- **Field objective:** Fly a timed straight leg at cruise on your own airframe and log pack current and voltage against your predicted cruise power.
- **Owner:** Propulsion & Power
- **Predict:** Predict cruise power and stall speed from your own ledger, not from the course design point of 60 W.
- **Weather fallback:** A crosswind ruins a timed leg. Fly the leg into wind and back out, average the two, and say in the log that you did.
- **Safety:** The first flight of any airframe is flown by the instructor. Your team's test pilot takes it on the second flight, never the first.
