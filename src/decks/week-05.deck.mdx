# The battery sets the mass

Week 5 · SLOP2440 · Bez Toomath

---

## Why this lecture is in week 5

Most courses teach power in week 9, next to the build.

By week 9 you have already drawn a wing.

The wing was sized for a mass. A third of that mass is the pack. You cannot
size the pack without knowing cruise power, and you cannot know cruise power
without the wing.

**So we open the loop here, with an estimate, and close it with a measurement.**

---

## The loop

```
wing area  →  stall speed  →  cruise speed
     ↑                              ↓
   mass    ←   pack size   ←   cruise power
```

It is circular. That is not a flaw in the method; it is the method.

You go round it three times this semester: in week 4 with guesses, in week 5
with bench numbers, and in week 11 with a flight log.

---

## Cruise power, from four numbers

```
P_elec  =  (W / (L/D)) * (V / eta)  +  P_avionics
```

- weight, in newtons, from the ledger
- lift-to-drag, which week 4 measured off a club trainer
- cruise speed, which you chose
- drive efficiency, which is the one you will be optimistic about

At the course design point this comes out at **60 W**.

---

## Be pessimistic about efficiency

ESC × motor × propeller.

Each one is "about ninety per cent" in the catalogue. Multiply three optimistic
numbers and you get one very optimistic number.

The course design point assumes half. Bench measurement in this week's studio
tells you what yours actually is.

{/* _class: quote */}

> A drive efficiency you did not measure is a wish with a decimal point.

---

## Sizing the pack

Four cells, 2200 mAh, nominal 14.8 volts.

- pack energy: **32.6 Wh**
- usable above the cut-off, at eighty per cent: **26 Wh**
- at 60 W cruise: **26 minutes**

`R-06` wants at least 26 Wh usable. `R-07` wants at least twenty minutes.

Both are met — at the design point, on paper, on a new pack, at twenty degrees.

---

## What a pack actually does

Nothing above is true of a pack that is cold, old, or being asked for peak
current.

- voltage sags under load, and sag is where your cut-off arrives early
- capacity falls with cycles, and your pack is not new
- the last twenty per cent is not yours: taking it is how a pack puffs

This is why `R-06` is verified by discharge on the bench, not by the label.

---

## Radio, and the failsafe you will not test

The link is fine until it is not. `R-12` asks for 400 metres and a failsafe
that circles and descends.

Two failures, both common, both cheap to prevent:

1. A range check passed on the ground with the aircraft on the grass, where the
   antenna pattern flatters you.
2. A failsafe configured, never tested, and set to "hold last stick position" —
   which on a turning aircraft means a spiral into the ground.

**Test it deliberately, at altitude, in week 10.**

---

## The flight controller is a loop, not a brain

It reads a rate, compares it with the rate you asked for, and moves a surface.

Proportional, integral, derivative. Three numbers that decide whether your
aircraft feels solid, spongy, or like it is fighting you.

You tune them on a club trainer this week, and on your own airframe in week 11
— where the airframe has different inertia and all your numbers change.

---

## This week's studio

Motor and propeller on the field thrust stand. Fresh pack. Log thrust against
current, all the way to full throttle.

**Predict static thrust and peak current before the stand is switched on.**
Both numbers go in the log first.

`R-04` wants static thrust at seven tenths of all-up weight. At the design point
that is **1100 g** of thrust against **1560 g** of aircraft.
